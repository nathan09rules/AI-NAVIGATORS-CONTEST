SYMPTOMS

List of all symptoms and their descriptions

Phlegm

Phlegm is the thick, sticky mucus that your body makes, especially when you're sick. It comes from your lungs and throat and is usually clear but can turn yellow, green, or brown if you have an infection. Your body makes phlegm to trap dust, germs, and other unwanted stuff, helping to keep your lungs clean.

Stomach Pain

Stomach pain is any discomfort or ache felt in the belly area. It can be mild or severe, sharp or dull, and may come and go. Common causes include indigestion, gas, infections, or cramps. Sometimes, it happens after eating certain foods or when feeling stressed. While most stomach pain goes away on its own, lasting or strong pain could mean something more serious, like an infection or organ problem. If the pain is very bad, doesn’t stop, or comes with vomiting or fever, it’s best to see a doctor.

Nausea

Nausea is the feeling that you might throw up, like your stomach is upset or uneasy. It can happen due to spoiled food, motion sickness, infections, or stress. Sometimes, nausea goes away on its own, but drinking water, resting, or deep breathing can help. If it lasts long or comes with severe pain or dehydration, see a doctor.

Burning Micturition

Burning micturition means feeling a burning or painful sensation while urinating. This can be due to a urinary tract infection (UTI), dehydration, kidney stones, or irritation from certain foods and drinks. Drinking plenty of water and maintaining good hygiene can help, but if the pain continues or comes with fever, blood in urine, or frequent urges to urinate, see a doctor.

Diarrhoea

Diarrhea is when you have loose or watery stools more often than usual. It can happen due to infections, food poisoning, stress, or medical conditions. Mild cases often go away on their own, but drinking plenty of water is important to prevent dehydration. If diarrhea lasts more than a few days, has blood, or comes with severe pain or fever, it’s best to see a doctor.
